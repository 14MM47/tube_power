## Q1. What patents has Vortex Bladeless (and its predecessor Deutecno) filed, and what do they claim?

### Takeaway
There are about five patent families, with priority dates from 2010 to 2017. Two of them predate the company and were filed by Deutecno S.L. and Abaccus. The 2010 family (WO2012017106 / EP2602483 / US9444372) disclosed the original idea: a cantilevered, tapered mast whose natural frequency is tuned to the vortex-shedding frequency, with power taken off by **piezo/ferroelectric** material and active tuning by modulating voltage. From 2014 the company moved to **permanent-magnet repulsion "tuning"** (WO2016055370 / EP3204635B1 / US10641243B2) and to a **coil-magnet (linear-alternator-type) generator in the mast or base** (WO2017174685 / EP3440344 / US11053914B2; WO2017174161; WO2018149942). Two of these are confirmed granted: US10641243B2 (May 2020) and US11053914B2 (July 2021). EP3204635B1 was also granted (May 2020). The first EP application (EP2602483) is listed as withdrawn.

- Physics stated in the description: the shedding frequency follows the Strouhal relation (Fv = S·V/h). The natural frequency of a cantilever's nth mode depends on I (second moment of area), E (Young's modulus), mass per unit length and a damping constant. Resonance happens when fn = Fv. Drag is described as "non-usable" and the alternating lift as the "usable force". — [EP2602483A1 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/5d/f7/20/770159c2c1200b/EP2602483A1.pdf)
- Tuning method in this first patent: active, not magnetic. An "electronic unit … manage[s] a control loop where the output is the value of the voltage" applied to the electromechanically coupled material, which changes the mast's effective Young's modulus. The input is wind speed, taken from an anemometer "or even better by using the stationary torsion of the wind turbine mast generated by the drag force". — [EP2602483A1 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/5d/f7/20/770159c2c1200b/EP2602483A1.pdf)
- Tapered-mast geometry, worked example: the mast diameter grows with height to follow Hellmann's wind-shear law, so vortices shed in sync along the whole length. Example: a **4 m mast tuned to 8 Hz** has radius **82.9 mm at 1 m height and 105.3 mm at 4 m**, for Hellmann exponent 0.16 and a mean wind of **6.5 m/s at 10 m**. The base is a heavy concrete foundation with a sealed compartment holding the control and regulation electronics. The mast is anchored at an "anchoring area" so the top "oscillate[s] freely with no other constraint than the elasticity of the material". — [EP2602483A1 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/5d/f7/20/770159c2c1200b/EP2602483A1.pdf)

It is characterised by vortices appearing "in a synchronised manner along the entire length" and by the control unit performing "an active modulation of the natural frequency of oscillation of the capturing element, matching or synchronising it with the frequency at which vortices appear". Claim 2 covers a mast whose section and geometry deliberately turn laminar flow into vortices. — [EP2602483A1 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/5d/f7/20/770159c2c1200b/EP2602483A1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Over time the company's design has moved from piezoelectric transduction with active electronic stiffness modulation (2010) to passive permanent-magnet repulsion that adds a deflection-dependent restoring stiffness (2014). It then moved to an electromagnetic converter with ring magnets and coils placed up the mast (2016–2017). For a home builder, the 2014–2017 families describe the architecture that was actually built: a flexible rod pivot, repelling magnets around the rod, and a coil/magnet generator at the base.
- Patent terms run 20 years from the filing date if maintenance fees are paid. Rough expiry: US9444372 around 2031 (PCT filed 2011-08-01). EP3204635B1 and US10641243B2 around Oct 2035. US11053914B2 / EP3440344 around Apr 2037. These are estimates, not register checks. EP2602483 is withdrawn, so the 2010 piezo concept has no EP protection, and its 2012–2013 publication is prior art.
- Patent infringement generally requires commercial or industrial activity in the EU and UK, where private non-commercial use is exempt. The US has no broad statutory private-use exemption. A one-off hobby build is therefore low-risk in Europe, while selling kits or units based on the magnetic-tuning or in-mast-generator claims could raise freedom-to-operate questions until the 2030s. This is general legal context, not a legal opinion.

## Q2. What has Vortex published technically (white papers, papers, EU project reports, product specs)?

### Takeaway
The main technical disclosure is the **"VIV resonant wind generators" green paper** by David J. Yáñez Villarreal, dated 7 June 2018 and hosted on the Vortex site and ResearchGate. It describes the mass-on-elastic-rod model, magnetic tuning, decoupling in high winds, the stator/oscillator ring layout of the alternator, and a 1 m wind-tunnel model on a carbon-fibre rod. EU support came through Horizon 2020 SME Instrument Phase 2 project **VORTEX (grant 726776)**, which started 1 June 2016 and ran 24 months. Public product figures: Nano about 1 m and 3 W; Tacoma 2.75 m, 100 W, about 15 kg; Atlantis/Grand 9–13 m and about 1 kW (prototype). Cut-in is about 3 m/s.

### Inferences
- A home builder can take a scalable parametric recipe from these documents. The mast is a light, stiff, hollow cylinder (diameter sets the shedding frequency, f ≈ 0.2·V/D). It sits on an elastic rod, carbon or glass-fibre pultrusion, whose bending stiffness together with the mast's moment of inertia sets fn. Ring magnets around the rod repel a magnet ring on the mast to add stiffness that grows with deflection, which broadens lock-in. A coil/magnet transducer at the base, or some way up the mast, extracts power. The 2010 patent's numbers (a 4 m mast at 8 Hz, radius 83–105 mm) show that mast diameter and frequency must be chosen together for the design wind speed: with S ≈ 0.2 and D ≈ 0.19 m, 8 Hz corresponds to about 7.6 m/s.
- Vortex's published power figures (3 W at 1 m, 100 W at 2.75 m) are nominal ratings, and no power curves were published. Treat them as upper bounds.

## Q3. What independent technical analyses of the Vortex design exist?

### Takeaway
The most cited independent study is the University of Glasgow wake-oscillator modelling paper (*Renewable Energy*, 2025, with heavy press coverage in 2026). It found a **peak efficiency of about 6%** (long masts), a **maximum of about 600 W** (structurally unsafe), and about **460 W as the best structurally safe design** (an 80 cm mast with a 65 cm diameter). All of these are modelled mechanical power, not electrical output. Several MDPI and *Scientific Reports* papers model tunable VIV bladeless turbines, and they agree that power collapses outside lock-in unless stiffness is tuned.

### Cited Findings
- Glasgow study: "Performance analysis and geometric optimisation of bladeless wind turbines using wake oscillator model", *Renewable Energy* (June 2025). — [ScienceDirect S096014812501211X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096014812501211X); [University of Glasgow news](https://www.gla.ac.uk/news/headline_1187739_en.html)
- Result: "maximum power (600 Watts) and peak efficiency (6%) are achieved through distinct geometric configurations". The highest-efficiency designs were the longest masts, "peaking at 6 percent", but they produced less total power. — [ScienceDirect (snippet)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096014812501211X); [Ecoticias, 2026](https://www.ecoticias.com/en/a-bladeless-wind-turbine-that-sways-in-the-wind-could-safely-produce-460-watts-in-computer-simulations-and-the-most-efficient-design-turned-out-not-to-be-the-most-powerful/53690/)
- Optimal safe design: an "80cm mast which is 65cm in diameter", up to **460 W**, against about 100 W for existing prototypes. Designs above 600 W "would sacrifice structural safety". — [CompositesWorld](https://www.compositesworld.com/news/university-research-studies-potential-of-bladeless-wind-turbines); [ACMA](https://acmanet.org/bladeless-wind-turbines-offer-potential-for-efficient-renewable-energy-production/)
- Caveats: "460 watts is modeled mechanical power… The study never wired its mast to a generator". It reports about **114.67 mW mean power at 3.6 m/s**, and about **140 Wh/yr** gross with wind-speed-dependent tuning against about **28 Wh/yr** untuned. — [MDPI Applied Sciences 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/22/11286)
- Other 2026 work: a cylindrical-cam VIV bladeless turbine (*Energies* 19(4):1090) — [doi:10.3390/en19041090](https://doi.org/10.3390/en19041090). An ANSYS/MATLAB mast natural-frequency study — [Al-Jabal Academy Journal 2026](https://lajak.edu.ly/journals/index.php/aajpas/article/download/434/74/1412). A columnar-cactus mast — [PMC12564873](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12564873/).
- An unsourced claim of "below 5% efficiency outside lock-in" appears on an AI wiki and should not be relied on. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Vortex_Bladeless)

### Inferences
- Independent modelling agrees that a VIV mast captures a small fraction of the wind power passing through its frontal area: about 6% at best in the Glasgow model, against roughly 35–45% for small rotors. Electrical output will be lower still once alternator losses are included. The 2026 *Applied Sciences* result (about 0.1 W at 3.6 m/s for a small rig) shows how small the output is at hobby scale.
- Frequency tuning (magnetic, a variable rod length, or a tuned mass) is the main lever. Untuned masts lose most of their annual energy because lock-in covers only a narrow wind band.

### Gaps
- No DTU or other national-lab test of a real Vortex unit was found. No independent measured power curve for Nano or Tacoma was found.
- The user's description of a "2026 modelling study showing ~6% energy capture" matches the Glasgow paper, which was published in June 2025 and covered heavily in 2026. No separate 2026 study with a 6% figure was found.

## Q4. Other inventors' patents on VIV / galloping / oscillating wind generators (and expired ones)

### Takeaway
The main non-Vortex families are Northeastern University's (inventor Mohammad Taslim) **"Non-rotating wind energy generator"** (US9222465B2, US9447774, WO2012142563A1), which uses vortex shedding and transverse galloping with linear alternators. **US10982648B2** (an individual applicant's telescoping natural-frequency tuning, granted 2021) is reported **expired for non-payment of fees**. Saphon Energy's Tunisian sail device and Katrick Technologies' oscillating-aerofoil "Wind Panel" are oscillating but not VIV masts.

### Cited Findings
- **Northeastern University / Taslim**: **US 9,222,465 B2** "Non-rotating wind energy generator". The abstract describes vortex shedding and transverse galloping driving "oscillatory, linear motion of a beam", with linear alternators producing power. — [RPX Insight US9222465B2](https://insight.rpxcorp.com/patent/US9222465B2); [USPTO PDF 9222465](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9222465); [Northeastern CoE news](https://coe.northeastern.edu/news/taslim-receives-patent-for-non-rotating-wind-energy-generator/)

Related publications: **US20160076521A1** (17 Mar 2016), **US20140175800A1**, and **WO2012142563A1** (inventors include Taslim, Thorp and Byers). The work was also SBIR-funded ("Non-rotating Wind Energy Generator"). — [USPTO PDF 9447774](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9447774); [Patexia US20160076521](https://patexia.com/us/publication/20160076521); [US20140175800A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140175800A1/en); [WO2012142563A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2012142563A1); [SBIR award](https://www.sbir.gov/node/1431)

Output claims are company-only. — [Katrick "Our Technologies/patents"](https://www.katricktechnologies.com/our-patents); [Sustainability Magazine](https://sustainabilitymag.com/renewable-energy/katrick-technologies-green-energy-innovation-with-wind-tech); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/katrick-technologies)

- Chinese record **CN115788768B** appeared in a bladeless natural-frequency-tuning search. Its content is unverified. — [UTB catalogue/Espacenet record](https://vufind.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-epo_espacenet_CN115788768BB3)

### Inferences
- For a hobbyist, the useful prior art is public and largely unencumbered. Vortex's EP2602483 (withdrawn) disclosed the basic tuned-VIV-mast concept in 2012–2013. US10982648 (telescoping stiffness tuning) has lapsed. Northeastern's 2012-priority families would expire by about 2032 even if maintained, and are US-only. Making something for personal use is broadly outside infringement in Europe, though not formally in the US.
- The Northeastern patents are the closest non-Vortex disclosure of a **bluff-body beam with linear alternators using both VIV and galloping**. A galloping cross-section such as a square or D-section can give a wider operating band than a pure VIV cylinder.

### Gaps

- Maintenance status of US9222465 and US9447774 is unknown. Saphon's international patent number was not found. Katrick's patent numbers were not retrieved.
- No pre-2006 (now expired) VIV wind-generator patents were identified in the time available.
