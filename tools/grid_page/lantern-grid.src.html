Designs

Source. The full optimisation grids in the tube_power repository (designs/lantern-250/calculator-output/grid/), produced by tools/lantern_calc.py sweep --csv. For one design at every wind speed from 1 to 40 m/s, run tools/lantern_calc.py envelope.

Fixed settings. 1 m tube; 5208 gimbal generator on a 100 mm belt arc; tuned 2–7 m/s, power capped from 7 m/s, braked from 8 m/s. The 25 m/s grids use configuration B's hub (built for 150 N·m, 12 mm axle, bushings 150 mm apart); the 40 m/s grids use configuration C's (350 N·m, 16 mm axle, 200 mm apart). End discs are built like the shell. The neck tube is the lightest standard carbon tube that passes.

A design passes when its Reynolds number at 7 m/s is at most 120,000 (the tested range of the evidence); it survives the storm requirement parked and braked (neck, skin, shell-wall ovalling, end-disc gust lift, hub); its swinging loads stay within limits up to the brake speed (neck fatigue and peak stress, skin, voltage, generator heat); and the generator can supply the best damping.

Galloping versions. Lantern 330-G is a 330 mm square prism that gallops instead of shedding vortices: it generates from about 2.5 m/s, holds its 20 W rating by turning the prism a few degrees on its own axis from 15.5 to 40 m/s, and turns 45° to stop. Lantern 330-GH is built for the most power from 15 to 40 m/s: an e-bike hub motor on a heavy belt, a ±25° swing and heavier springs, no power cap and no pitch regulation (face-on, or feathered above 40 m/s); it averages about 150 W over that range but only starts at about 6 m/s. Its curve comes from tools/galloping_calc.py curve, a quasi-steady model with smooth-flow coefficients for a square; gusty air may reduce it substantially. Its yearly energy assumes 80% of the model output is achieved. Specification in designs/lantern-330-g/.

How to read the numbers. Efficiency is measured against the wind passing the tube's frontal area. The low case is half the model figure, allowing for gusty wind. Yearly energy assumes a Rayleigh wind distribution with a 4.5 m/s mean at hub height and half the swinging time locked on. The benefit of end discs comes from an assumed end-loss model.
